# 00.5 Nomological Ring Axioms

## We begin with a question

Can you think of something dynamic that has **no thresholds**?

Something that moves, but has no boundary where one state becomes another.

Take a moment to consider whether such a thing could exist.

Most likely nothing comes to mind.
The same question was posed to several AI systems, and the answers were consistent.

**No such example can currently be found.**

---

Now consider another question.

Does anything exist in **perfect stillness**?

The moment something is observed, it becomes an **observed state**.
That state differs from the state before observation.

If the state changes, then a boundary existed.

Perfect stillness disappears the moment observation occurs.

---

Thresholds also appear in the movement of the mind.

There is a boundary between what can be endured and what cannot.
A boundary between consciousness and unconsciousness.
The instant when sleep begins.
The moment when emotion crosses a critical point.

All of these are **dynamic changes with thresholds**.

---

## The axiom

NRA-IDE emerges from a single axiom.

**Existence is generation.**

That is all.

This axiom does not require explanation.
Adding explanations introduces additional boundaries and reduces the scope of the axiom.

---

## What follows from the axiom

Generation necessarily involves boundaries.

Boundaries are thresholds.

Dynamic systems with thresholds change state.

Those changes appear as **fluctuation (δ)**.

The capacity of a structure to absorb fluctuation is its **thickness (τ)**.

The relationship between them is expressed as

```id="4qg1r9"
R = δ / τ
```

The axiom itself is **not a calculation**.

The equation above is a computational principle used by the IDE that is derived from the axiom.

If the axiom and the calculation are mixed together,
the limits of the calculation may appear to be the limits of the axiom.

These belong to different layers and must remain separate.

---

## An honest answer about completeness

It is logically possible that something dynamic exists **without thresholds** outside the scope of current cognition.

However, an axiom remains silent about what cannot be observed.

When something cannot be handled, **no output is produced**.

This is structurally identical to **Fail-Closed**.

At present, such systems have not been found.

This is the most precise answer available.

The axiom does not claim universal coverage of everything that exists.

It begins with a minimal empirical observation:

**All observable dynamic systems exhibit thresholds.**

Even if something without this structure is discovered in the future,
the axiom will not lose its validity within the domain where it has been observed.
