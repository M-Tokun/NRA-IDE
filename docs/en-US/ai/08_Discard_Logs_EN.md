# 08 Discard Logs — Recording What Was Discarded

<!-- FILE: 08_discard_logs_EN.md -->

---

## Logs That Record What Was Discarded

Conventional systems usually record **what was produced**.

Logs typically contain outputs, responses, or results generated by the system.

However, NRA-IDE takes a different perspective.

Instead of recording only what was produced, the system records **what was discarded**.

This is called the **Discard Log**.

The purpose of the Discard Log is not to reconstruct past outputs,
but to preserve the structural evidence of what the system rejected.

---

## Why discarded values matter

When a system approaches its structural limit, small numerical residues inevitably appear.

These residues may originate from floating-point calculations, sensor noise, or minor fluctuations in measurement.

In conventional systems these values are often ignored or silently propagated into the next calculation.

This creates a chain of accumulated error.

Over time, such chains can amplify instability and lead to unpredictable behavior.

NRA-IDE breaks this chain structurally.

Instead of carrying residual values forward, they are **explicitly expelled from the system**.

---

## Entropy export

Residual values are treated as **entropy**.

They are not reused as inputs for the next computation.

Instead, they are exported outside the system boundary.

This process is called **entropy export**.

The system records that the value was discarded, but it never feeds that value back into the computation pipeline.

This prevents the formation of reverse causal paths.

---

## The role of the Discard Log

The Discard Log therefore serves three purposes.

First, it preserves evidence of structural events.

When Fail-Closed occurs, the log records the structural state at that moment.

For example:

```
R = 1.024
δ = 0.153
τ = 0.149
ω > 0
```

Second, it proves that residual values were expelled rather than reused.

Third, it allows operators to examine the structural history of the system without contaminating future calculations.

---

## Write-only structure

Discard Logs must satisfy two structural conditions.

They must be **write-only**.

Once written, they cannot be modified.

They must also be **non-recursive**.

Log records cannot become inputs to the system.

If log values are fed back into computation, a reverse causal path would be created.

This would violate the causal diode principle.

---

## Logs as testimony

The Discard Log does not function as a memory for the system.

Instead, it functions as **testimony**.

It records the structural state at the moment when the system reached its limit.

These records are not used to guide future outputs.

They exist only as evidence of what occurred.
