## On Derivatives



The MIT License permits commercial use of derivatives. This is not in conflict with the developer's intent. There is, however, one personal wish the developer would like to put on record.



I do not want derivatives of this framework to be closed off for the exclusive benefit of any particular individual, company, or organization.



My honest hope is that useful technology reaches as many people as possible, as quickly as possible. I ask that the core logic and sole axiom not be isolated from others through patents. Even in commercial use, I hope that consideration will be given to ensuring the benefits of this technology reach not only people living today, but future generations as well.

This is a nonbinding personal request. It does not add a license condition, restrict the permissions granted by the MIT License, determine patent validity or ownership, or constitute legal advice. Any binding restriction would require a separate, explicit licensing or legal decision.



Unless a situation arises in which this framework is misused or causes clear harm to humanity, I have no intention of filing patents myself.

## On Humanitarian Use



The design principle of this framework is to support safety-oriented understanding and evaluation through structural integrity. NRA-IDE does not independently guarantee safety, fitness for a particular purpose, or valid operation in a specific domain. In domains directly connected to human life — medicine, life support, social infrastructure, safety engineering — I hope carefully validated uses of this framework will be available without technical or economic barriers.

## On Research and Development



Research, theoretical extension, verification, and refutation based on this framework are all welcome. This framework is not a finished doctrine. It is offered as an open foundation for inquiry. I hope that knowledge gained from it will be returned to the world in as open a form as possible.

A research extension does not become a new axiom, canonical definition, or conforming implementation merely by being proposed, published, implemented, or placed in this repository. Canonical change requires explicit maintainer acceptance and synchronized updates under the canonical precedence rules; it must not introduce a second or subsequent axiom.
