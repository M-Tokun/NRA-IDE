"Sudden Trip" Mechanics — Why It Happens Suddenly
PhenomenonWhat HappensPrecursors Detected by EMA
Overload Current exceeds rated → Winding temp rises → Insulation breakdown τ narrows as current EMA begins accumulating
Phase Imbalance Current difference across 3 phases → concentrated on one winding → local burnout Detects rising EMA of imbalance ratio
Lock Current Start failure → 6–7× rated current flows continuously EMA instantly captures simultaneous surge across all phases
Phase Loss 1-phase open → 1.7× current concentrated on remaining 2 phases Imbalance instantly hits 100% → fastest SILENCE trigger

"Sudden Trip" in mechanical systems is different from molecular reactions.
Thermal breakdown of insulation occurs only after temperature accumulates — but the breakdown itself is instantaneous.
EMA remembers the "momentum of accumulation" and can structurally
detect the "gradual buildup phase" before breakdown occurs.
